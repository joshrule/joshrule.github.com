Josh Rule · Why we lack financial peace and how to get it

· GitHub · Scholar

The average US resident is extremely wealthy from a global and historical perspective. But, we lack the peace and security we imagine the wealthy ought to feel. More often, our finances sit at the center of many of our worries, anxieties, and fears.

While it might initially seem puzzling that we have both extreme material wealth and crippling financial fear, this situation makes sense when we adopt the Bible’s point-of-view. That isn’t to say that God wants us to live in constant financial fear. No, the point is that the prevailing western views of wealth and ownership fail to lead to financial peace because they approach ownership and wealth completely backward. We can find financial peace by learning what God has shared with us about how wealth and ownership really work.

In particular, the prevailing view teaches that wealth lies in what we own. In reality, we own nothing. God owns it all. At the same time, we are responsible for paying our bills, giving generously, and saving prudently. We resolve this tension by realizing that we are not owners ourselves but are instead agents acting on behalf of God, the true owner. As his agents, he gives us his authority to use his resources for his purposes. Everything we have access to in this life, God has given us so that we can use it for his purposes (not ours…his).

When we look at what God has said about his purposes, about the things that really matter to him in the long run—and which should matter to us, as his followers—we find that money and possessions have no eternal value. Instead, God wants us to be in right relationship with him and with all that he has made. These relationships are what we should value above all else—they are our true wealth. From this perspective, money is not wealth. Instead, money’s natural tendency is to destroy true wealth by stripping loving relationship out of the world and replacing it with impersonal power. But, we can choose another way. We can choose to use money for relationship building. We can fight money’s natural tendencies and find ways to use our spending, giving, saving, and investing—our entire financial lives—as acts of relationship building. We can use every possible resource to create a world in which everything is in right relationship with him and thus in right relationship with everything else.

The result of heeding God’s wisdom here is financial peace. When I no longer felt responsible for exerting control I did not have in pursuit of something which was not wealth, I was finally able to start putting money in its proper place. It became just one resource of many (e.g. time, intelligence, skill/talent) that I could use to build God-honoring relationships. God promises to provide all the resources we need to build those kinds of relationships. The more I learn to trust this promise of provision, the more financial peace I experience. The same is true for you. We don’t have to worry about the resources; we just need to use whatever resources God provides to do what we believe is best for building godly relationships. We can rest in the fact that God owns absolutely everything, and from that vast storehouse, he will give us what we need if we are doing the work he has given us to do.

If these ideas are compelling to you, take your next step in pursuing financial peace.
