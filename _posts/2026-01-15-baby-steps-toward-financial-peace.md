title: "Baby steps toward financial peace"
---

If you've been reading through the articles posted here about financial
peace, you might be wondering what to do next. That is, you might be
wondering how to take this information and use it to begin living a life
of financial peace.

If you're not sure if you believe what you've read or even understand
how all the ideas fit together, that's okay. Your first step may just be
to keep reading and pondering this material, meditating on it, talking
about it with others, and slowly letting the ideas come together into a
new way of thinking about wealth and ownership. That is good, meaningful
work in and of itself, and there's no need to rush it.

Once you are beginning to feel confident in how these ideas work
together, here are three simple ways you can begin to put them into
practice:

**Put it to the test.** Even if you aren't sure you believe this way of
thinking about financial peace is true, you can try it out yourself. You
can get your own data and draw your own conclusion. Use some money to
build a relationship this week (take someone out for coffee, buy a meal
for a homeless person and talk with them), and use some to
transactionally leverage power (take an uber without talking to the
driver, order doordash and force them to just leave it at the door). See
which feels better.

**Give Regularly.** If you are new to following Jesus and haven't built
the habit of charitable giving, I would encourage you to start giving
regularly in some way. Giving to the work being done through a local
church would be great, but it doesn't have to be there if you're not
sure you've found your long-term church family yet. It could be giving
to a local soup kitchen or to a humanitarian aid organization like World
Vision. But, step into giving as a habit, not as a one-off thing you do
when you feel like it. Make relationship building a regular thing. Doing
so has been a game-changer for me, because it really does help me see
God's abundant generosity toward me, and it helps me direct that
generosity toward building better relationships with other people. Even
if I never meet them, I've done something to help them and to tangibly
share God's love.

**Review your spending.** If you want to go a little deeper with these
ideas and do a little work on your own, grab your spending record for
the last month and just work through it line-by-line. Ask yourself
whether each expense leaned more toward building God-honoring loving
relationships, or toward leveraging impersonal power over others,
perhaps exploiting the vulnerable, or harming the planet God gave us to
steward. I can't say that this exercise will necessarily be fun, but it
will almost certainly be eye-opening and give you some ideas about how
you can pursue financial peace.
