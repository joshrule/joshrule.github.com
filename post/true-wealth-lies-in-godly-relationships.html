Josh Rule · True wealth lies in godly relationships

· GitHub · Scholar

As God’s agents, God gives us his authority to use his things for his purposes. To do that well, it would really help to know what his purposes are. I mean, what are God’s purposes?

For this article, it’s enough to say that God tells us his purposes for us in The Bible. One great way to summarize what The Bible says is that God wants us to live lives of peace.

We all have different ideas about what peace looks like. Perhaps it’s a quiet life in the country, or just curling up on the couch with a book, forever. Or maybe peace means being the one who defuses the drama around you so that conflicts fizzle out. Or, maybe it is a life of pacifism or anti-war activism.

The Bible’s view of peace, however, comes from the Hebrew word shalom. And, this idea of shalom is so central to the biblical view of the good life that for millennia, it has been a common greeting. Where we say hello, the biblical authors would say, “Peace”. The greeting is both a blessing and an expression of deep desire, a way of saying, “Peace to you and yours. That is what we want for this world.” Now, shalom isn’t just shutting away the noise and conflict, or absorbing and defusing it, or working to end it. It is instead a deeper and better kind of peace. Shalom is when everything is in right relationship with everything else because it is in right relationship with God. It is what happens when every interaction is good and right, where instead of tearing down and dismantling, everything is building upon everything else, where every action is a net good rather than a net harm.

So, when I say that God wants his people to live lives of peace, what I mean is this. He wants us to be people whose lives are devoted to living in right relationship with God and with everything that he has made.

The book “When Helping Hurts”, by Steve Corbett and Brian Fikkert, presents a helpful way of thinking about the kinds of relationships humans can form. In particular, they note that humans participate in four major kinds of relationships: first and foremost a relationship with God, as well as relationships with ourselves, with other people, and with all non-human creation.

Living lives of peace is living lives devoted to lovingly and generously building these four relationships.

We see that clearly throughout the Bible, but to be concrete, consider the example of Genesis 1–3.

God prepared a garden for humanity, gave them meaning and purpose, had conversations with them and walked together with them. It’s a picture of relational intimacy between us and God.

We also see that God created Adam and Eve to work together because it wasn’t good for a human to work alone. And, the first relationship between people was a marriage—one of the most profound and life-giving relationships possible. That’s our introduction to relationships in the Bible. Also, God instructed this couple to fill the earth with new people. Building relationships between people is part of the mission God gave us.

This passage also has a lot to say about our relationship with ourselves. Adam and Eve were so comfortable with who God made them to be that they didn’t need clothes and confidently interacted directly with God and worked alongside him. It’s only after choosing to ignore God and go their own way that they begin to feel shame and to hide themselves from God and from each other.

Finally, God placed humanity in a small garden, a place of order of beauty, and told them to work it and keep it, and to subdue the rest of the world, extending the garden into the wild places, cultivating the entire planet into a place of beauty and abundance. For anyone who has spent time trying to cultivate and nurture living things, whether children, pets, a garden, or even just a front lawn, it quickly becomes clear that cultivating life is hard work. It requires a deep understanding of the life you are trying to grow, and a genuine care for it.

To sum up, God’s purpose for us is to live a life in pursuit of peace, which means seek right relationship with God and all that he has made. Walking with God in this way is where true wealth lies. More pointedly, God gives you money so you can build these relationships. They are what the Bible teaches us to value above all else.
