title: "Jesus' promised provision enables financial peace"
---

Even after recognizing that [God owns everything and we are own nothing]({%
post_url 2026-01-15-how-ownership-works-we-are-gods-agents %}) and that [true
wealth lies not in money but in godly relationships]({% post_url
2026-01-15-how-wealth-works-pursue-right-relationship-rather-than-money %}), it
might still be unclear how those truths are supposed to provide financial peace
and security.

Instead, our first reaction might be that a biblical view of wealth and
ownership weakens our chances of financial peace rather than
strengthening them. Rather than providing a blueprint for accumulating
enough money to pay off our debts, achieve financial independence, or
buy a private jet, God seems to charge us to give time and money away
toward building relationships. If we're already stretched to the limit
just to make ends meet, the idea that we should be directing our money
toward our relationships with God and his creation might seem like a
step too far.

If that resonates, let me clarify two things.

First, consider the source. It's not just some guy on the Internet
telling you to do this. Jesus is telling you to do this. So, if you
follow Jesus, let that have the appropriate weight it should have in
your life. If you aren't committed to following Jesus, I would still
suggest that you think about these ideas. Jesus is an incredibly smart
guy. The more I test what he has to say, the more I enjoy my life. So,
that's the first thing. Consider the source.

Second, it is too much. Building all these relationships is a lot of
work, too much for one person. But, that's kinda the point. We're not
using our own to do it, because we don't own anything. Even our own
strength is strength that God has given us! We're not the authority
here. Jesus is. He has to be the one to provide the resources to do all
this work, and that's just what he promises to do.

In Matthew 6, after telling us that our hearts---our hopes and joys and
fears---follow the things we treasure, he then tells us not to worry
about food and clothing and money. Now, a working family of four in his
original audience probably made, on average, \$800 in today's dollars.
Today, we would say that they were in extreme poverty. They didn't have
central heat or air conditioning. They relied on their clothing to
protect them from the sun and from the cold. Many were agrarian and were
just one failed crop away from starving. Yet, Jesus tells these people
not to worry about clothes, or food, or money. Instead, he says, "For
the Gentiles \[that is, people who aren't following God\] eagerly seek
all these things; for your heavenly Father knows that you need all these
things. But seek first His kingdom and His righteousness, and all these
things will be provided to you."

This verse has been a constant companion of mine for more than decade,
because it is such an incredible promise. It tells us that God knows our
needs, and that we don't need to worry about them. As long as we focus
on the job he has given us, using his resources on behalf of his
kingdom, pursuing shalom through godly relationships---as long as we
focus on doing that, he will provide the resources we need to do it.

There's the peace! There it is. We don\'t have to worry about where the
resources are going to come from. We just focus on doing what we believe
is best for building godly relationships. If we do that, he will
provide. It might be a good job, or a check in the mail, or a friend who
can fix that busted pipe, or a thousand other things. However he does
it, God will be faithful to his promises.

Financial peace, then, doesn't come only after our bank account hits
some target number or only once we've secured our family's financial
future. It is less about working to change our local situation---zooming
in so that all we see is our bank account and bills---and more about
realizing our global situation---God is good, he is in control, and he
promises to provide for us as we walk with him. We can rest in the fact
that God owns absolutely everything, and from that vast storehouse, he
will give us what we need if we are doing the work he has given us to
do. In that, we can find security from worry and anxiety and peace as we
pursue right relationship with God and all that he has made.
