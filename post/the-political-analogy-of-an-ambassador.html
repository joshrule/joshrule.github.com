Josh Rule · The political analogy of an ambassador

· GitHub · Scholar

Paul, an early missionary, wrote a letter to the church in Corinthian, a city in Asia Minor. It’s now the Book of 2 Corinthians in the Bible. In it, he calls us “ambassadors” for Christ.

More specifically, he says, “Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making an appeal through us; we beg you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God,” (2 Corinthians 5:20). Now, when you come to a “therefore”, it’s best to heed the old saying and ask yourself what the “therefore” is there for. Backing up a few sentences, Paul says that when we follow Jesus, we are reconciled to God. That is, following Jesus heals the relational break we create when we choose to walk away from God. This relational reconciliation is a great thing because it is in relationship with God that we find our greatest happiness. As part of this healed relationship, God has entrusted us with the job of sharing the good news that we can all be reconciled with God. The “therefore” is there to show that it is in this context that Paul calls followers of Jesus ambassadors for Jesus.

This passage will be more helpful if we take a moment to understand what exactly it means to be an ambassador (Hint: It means much more than you might think given the way that it is used most commonly today, where the term “brand ambassador” describes people who are effectively just marketing affiliates.). Years ago, my wife worked for the US State Department. We were posted at embassies overseas and had occasion to meet the US ambassador to the countries where we worked. And these ambassadors’ primary job was to promote US interests in a foreign land. That’s what it means to be an ambassador. You have no authority of your own. You take on someone else’s authority and their resources and their goals as if they were your own, and then you go and do what they would do if they were there. Most of what the US accomplishes abroad isn’t accomplished directly by the President or Secretary of State. It instead accomplished by proxy—delegating the power and resources of the entire federal government to ambassadors who use it to effect change all over the world. So, an ambassador is someone who acts on behalf of a political leader to bring their power and authority to bear on some issue to bring about the result that the leader desires to see.

Here, Paul is telling the church in Corinth that when we follow Jesus, we become his ambassadors. Now, God has quite a bit of authority as King of the Universe, and owning everything means that he has significant resources at his disposal. If we are his ambassadors, we inherit that authority and those resources. We also inherit God’s goals. We become his representatives, his agents, here on the earth, working with God as he realizes his kingdom here on earth.
