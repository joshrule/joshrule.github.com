title: US residents lack financial peace and security
---

Even though the average US American is extremely materially wealthy in
terms of the money we earn and the lifestyle we can afford, we often
lack financial peace and security.

Earning just \$6,610/yr in the US is enough to put you in the [top third
of global
earners](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/how-rich-am-i?income=6610&countryCode=USA&numAdults=1&numChildren=0)
(adjusting for cost of living differences around the world), and more
than [90% of Americans earn this
amount](https://dqydj.com/income-percentile-calculator/) each year. Even
so, the investment company Charles Schwab found in 2024 that [only 10%
of US residents feel
wealthy](https://pressroom.aboutschwab.com/press-releases/press-release/2024/Schwab-Survey-Reveals-That-Americans-Think-It-Takes-2.5-Million-to-Be-Considered-Wealthy-in-2024/default.aspx),
and Achieve reported in 2023 that only [11% of US residents have
achieved financial
freedom](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/only-1-in-10-americans-are-living-their-definition-of-financial-freedom-achieve-survey-finds-301906121.html),
according to their individual definitions of the term. More than 90% of
us are earning enough to be wealthy, while only about 10% of us feel
wealthy. This discrepancy alone is enough to demonstrate that we
generally lack financial peace and security.

Certainly some of this discrepancy can be explained through our
financial habits. [Bankrate
reports](https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/savings-account-average-balance/#key-saving-statistics)
that the median American household only has about \$8,000 in emergency
and retirement savings, and 19% have no emergency savings at all. What's
more, 33% (1 in 3) of households have more credit card debt than
emergency savings. [PNC
Bank](https://www.pnc.com/content/dam/pnc-com/pdf/corporateandinstitutional/organizational-financial-wellness/organizational-financial-wellness-workplace-report.pdf?msockid=208776d0f21b6d2c295163d5f3826c0b)
also reports that 67% of US workers are living paycheck to
paycheck---their expenses match or exceed their income.

It's difficult to reach any sort of financial security with such low
levels of savings, so it is little wonder that most of us feel financial
strain rather than financial peace. The [American Psychological
Association's Stress in America
surveys](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/stress) consistently
report concerns about money as among US Americans' top stressors.
[NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/data-money-stress-cope)
and [Ramsey
Solutions](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/budgeting/state-of-personal-finance)
both report that about half of US Americans worry regularly, even daily,
about money; [Thrive
Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Thriving-Wallet-Research-Insights-Report.pdf)
reports a much higher number, with 90% of US Americans agreeing that
money has a daily negative impact on their stress levels. The Ramsey
report further shows that around 43% are having trouble paying bills and
40% are losing sleep over money issues.

In short, US Americans are concerned about money, and often rightly so.
Healthy financial habits, however, are no guarantee of financial peace.
Even those who have a high net worth may find themselves worried about
money, while those with a low net worth may find themselves living lives
of financial peace. Net worth and financial peace simply aren't causally
connected, because ultimately, financial peace isn't rooted in the
numbers on our bank statements, but in a correct understanding of the
nature of wealth and ownership.
