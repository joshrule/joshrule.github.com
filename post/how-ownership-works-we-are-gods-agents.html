Josh Rule · How ownership works: We are God's agents

· GitHub · Scholar

We often believe that we own things, that we are lords and masters over our domains. In reality, we own nothing. We have no ultimate control over our money and possessions (or even our minds and bodies). And it’s not just that these things are subject to the whims of the bank, local government, or the global economy: humans simply don’t have ultimate control over anything. Instead, God owns everything. He retains ultimate control over everything he has made, including our money, careers, lives, minds, and bodies.

At the same time, we are responsible for paying our bills, giving generously, and saving prudently. Only rarely does God directly do these sorts of things for us, and the pattern of Scripture makes it clear that we should expect to bear these responsibilities ourselves. God gave us responsibility for managing the earth (Genesis 1:28; Hebrews 2:6–8) and we will be held responsible for our choices (2 Corinthians 5:9–10).

The result is major tension: we own nothing and yet bear many of the responsibilities of ownership. The Bible makes sense of this tension through a series of analogies. Each analogy is presented as a way of understanding this complex relationship between God, us, and our financial responsibilities. These analogies include: the religious analogy of an image; the political analogy of an ambassador; and the economic analogy of a steward. Taken together, they show that Give gives us real authority over what he has made, but it is not authority to act willy-nilly, doing whatever we want because it’s what we want. Instead, it’s authority to act as he would act. God gives us access to his resources so that we can use them to do what he wants done. These analogies, then, suggest that God is the owner, and we are his agents. We are not acting on our own, but on his behalf. We have God’s authority to use God’s resources for God’s purposes.
