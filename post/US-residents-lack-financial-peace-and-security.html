Josh Rule · US residents lack financial peace and security

· GitHub · Scholar

Even though the average US American is extremely materially wealthy in terms of the money we earn and the lifestyle we can afford, we often lack financial peace and security.

Earning just $6,610/yr in the US is enough to put you in the top third of global earners (adjusting for cost of living differences around the world), and more than 90% of Americans earn this amount each year. Even so, the investment company Charles Schwab found in 2024 that only 10% of US residents feel wealthy, and Achieve reported in 2023 that only 11% of US residents have achieved financial freedom, according to their individual definitions of the term. More than 90% of us are earning enough to be wealthy, while only about 10% of us feel wealthy. This discrepancy alone is enough to demonstrate that we generally lack financial peace and security.

Certainly some of this discrepancy can be explained through our financial habits. Bankrate reports that the median American household only has about $8,000 in emergency and retirement savings, and 19% have no emergency savings at all. What’s more, 33% (1 in 3) of households have more credit card debt than emergency savings. PNC Bank also reports that 67% of US workers are living paycheck to paycheck—their expenses match or exceed their income.

It’s difficult to reach any sort of financial security with such low levels of savings, so it is little wonder that most of us feel financial strain rather than financial peace. The American Psychological Association’s Stress in America surveys consistently report concerns about money as among US Americans’ top stressors. NerdWallet and Ramsey Solutions both report that about half of US Americans worry regularly, even daily, about money; Thrive Global reports a much higher number, with 90% of US Americans agreeing that money has a daily negative impact on their stress levels. The Ramsey report further shows that around 43% are having trouble paying bills and 40% are losing sleep over money issues.

In short, US Americans are concerned about money, and often rightly so. Healthy financial habits, however, are no guarantee of financial peace. Even those who have a high net worth may find themselves worried about money, while those with a low net worth may find themselves living lives of financial peace. Net worth and financial peace simply aren’t causally connected, because ultimately, financial peace isn’t rooted in the numbers on our bank statements, but in a correct understanding of the nature of wealth and ownership.
