Josh Rule · God owns everything

· GitHub · Scholar

God owns everything. The Bible is clear on this point from beginning to end. We own nothing, because God owns everything.

For example, Paul, an early missionary, wrote a letter to the church in Colossae, a city in Asia Minor. It’s now the Book of Colossians in the Bible. In it, Paul says the following about Jesus, “He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things were created through him and for him” (Colossians 1:15–16). In that first sentence, Paul establishes that Jesus is the best thing that’s ever existed. He is preeminent in all creation, the one with the rights of the firstborn son, granting him privileged legal and social status in ancient Jewish culture. In the second sentence, Paul says more about why Jesus is so special. He is special because he is the owner of everything, everywhere, and he is the owner of everything everywhere because he made it all. He is the author, so he is the one with the authority. What’s more, he not only made it, but he made it for himself. “[A]ll things were created through him and for him.” Jesus is the ultimate recipient of all that he made. He didn’t make it and sign it over to anyone else, certainly not you or me, or even humanity generally. Jesus owns it all.

It doesn’t stop there. Passages throughout the Bible that make it clear that our existence (Psalm 24:1, Proverbs 22:2, Psalm 139:13, 1 Corinthians 6:19–20), our health (Job), our abilities (Exodus 31:6, Romans 12:4–8), our vocation (Ephesians 2:10), the money we earn (Deuteronomy 8:18), our possessions (1 Chronicles 29:12,14), the days of our lives (Job 14:5; Psalm 90:3–6), and even the societies in which we live (Romans 13:1); God provided all of it and retains control over it.

We are not owners, and we will not feel at peace about our money and possessions if we continue to think of ourselves as owners. It’s like thinking of Voldemort as the hero in Harry Potter. You can do it if you want to, but you will never find peace down that road. It just doesn’t fit reality, so you will constantly find yourself at odds with reality. You’re trying to make something fit that will never fit.

The only way to work with reality here is to throw away the idea that our money, our stuff, our minds, our bodies, our lives are actually ours to do with as we please. They’re not. All of it is God’s to do with as he pleases. He owns everything.
