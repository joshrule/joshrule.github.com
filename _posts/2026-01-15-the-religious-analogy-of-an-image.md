title: "The religious analogy of an image"
---

Genesis 1:27 says, "God created humankind in his own image, in the image
of God he created them, male and female he created them." This passage
makes a profound statement about who we are and how we are to operate in
the world, but its significance is often downplayed or entirely ignored
by modern readers because we lack important cultural context.

In colloquial English, we might say that "Bob is the spitting image of
Tom," meaning that Bob looks exactly like Tom. That's not quite what's
being said here. It's not saying merely that humans look like God or
even that humanity looks like God.

Certainly, our being made in the image of God isn't a claim about our
visual appearance. God is pure spirit (John 4:24)---he doesn't have a
body for ours to look like. The claim that we are made in God's image
isn't a claim about walking on two feet or using opposable thumbs, much
less about being from a certain race or ethnicity.

Nor is the image of God merely about our capacities as humans. While
it's true that we, like God, use complex natural languages and have the
capacity for love, creativity, and free will, there's more to the idea
of being God's image than merely a family resemblance.

Instead, this term "image" (*tselem* or צֶלֶם) is used in other parts of
the Bible to refer to graven images, what we often call "idols". There
are actually several words that refer to these sorts of idols, but
"tselem" is one of them. In the cultures surrounding ancient Israel,
graven images were understood as being related to some deity. They
weren't the deity herself, but they were her representatives on earth,
with real authority to do what she wanted done. In fact, images were one
of the main ways ancient gods and goddesses were believed to spread
their way of doing things across the land.

By calling us images of God, Genesis is saying that we, humanity, are
God's representatives on earth. We have real authority to do what he
wants done. We aren't God himself, but we are one key way he spreads his
way of doing things across the land. Some of our capacities will thus be
similar to God's, because we need the ability to do the work he himself
would do. But, it's not the resemblance alone that's important. It's the
idea that we represent God and are on a mission to spread his values and
way of life that's essential. We are his images, his representatives,
[his agents]({% post_url 2026-01-15-how-ownership-works-we-are-gods-agents %}).
