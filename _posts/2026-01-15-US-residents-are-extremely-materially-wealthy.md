title: "US residents are extremely materially wealthy"
---

The average US resident is extremely materially wealthy, both globally and
historically.

We are materially wealthy *globally* speaking.

As of the writing of this article, Elon Musk is currently [the world's richest
individual](https://www.forbes.com/real-time-billionaires), with over $450B.
Perhaps you know that name and have opinions about how he ought to use that
money. Not many, however, know Serrin Dossa. [Bill Gates tells some of her
story](https://www.gatesnotes.com/search-reader?readerfocus=life-on-less-than-2-dollars-a-day):
she sells corn porridge in Lagos for less than $2 a day to support herself, her
husband, and three kids.

Few of us face these financial extremes, but where exactly do you think you sit
between Elon and Serrin?

Say you earn the [federal minimum wage of
$7.25/hr](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage) for 20hrs a week.
Working 50 weeks a year, you'd earn just $7,250. Even then, [Giving What We
Can](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org) estimates that you could afford a better
quality of life than [72 of every 100
people](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/how-rich-am-i?income=7250&countryCode=USA&numAdults=1&numChildren=0)
on the planet. With the [US median per capita income of
\$43,289](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/US/SEX255223), that
number jumps to [97 out of 100
people](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/how-rich-am-i?income=43289&countryCode=USA&numAdults=1&numChildren=0),
and at just \$62,883 each year, [you become part of the global
1%](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/how-rich-am-i?income=62883&countryCode=USA&numAdults=1&numChildren=0).
Perhaps these comparisons surprise you; I know I consistently underestimate how
materially wealthy the US is. Either way, the point remains that most US
residents are, *globally* speaking, extremely wealthy.

We are also materially wealthy *historically* speaking.

Eric Beinhocker estimates in [The Origin of
Wealth](https://www.google.com/search?hl=en&q=the%20origins%20of%20wealth%20beinhocker)
that the residents of the ancient Mediterranean---including the original
audience for Jesus' teaching---earned (adjusting for inflation) approximately
\$240 per person per year or \$0.65 per day. That would put them in [extreme
poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty), a far cry from our
modern wealth. Even just 200 years ago, roughly [80% of the
planet](https://ourworldindata.org/a-history-of-global-living-conditions#poverty)
lived in these conditions (today, [it's about
10%](https://unglobalcompact.org/what-is-gc/our-work/social/poverty), and still
falling).

Also, let's not forget that even the most privileged
elite of days gone by---including kings, queens, emperors, and
popes---didn't have access to the Internet, consumer electronics, modern
medicine, or even modern plumbing. Now, there's nuance to this argument,
which is perhaps one reason internet opinions are divided about whether
historical kings and queens had better lives than the average US
resident today (e.g.
[here](https://www.quora.com/Does-today-s-middle-class-have-a-better-standard-of-living-than-royalty-did-in-the-Middle-Ages),
[here](https://www.quora.com/How-was-the-quality-of-life-of-a-rich-ancient-Roman-or-medieval-European-compared-to-an-average-middle-class-modern-American),
[here](https://www.quora.com/Do-western-European-and-American-middle-class-live-better-today-than-wealthy-medieval-kings-lived),
and
[here](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskHistory/comments/unbnbh/who_has_a_better_quality_of_life_a_medieval_king)).
Royalty had staff do most of their work for them, and they often had
fiefdoms and empires to provide other resources. Even so, no staff could
replace modern medicine or access to information. On balance, the point
stands that most US residents are, *historically* speaking, extremely
wealthy.
