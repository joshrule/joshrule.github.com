title: "Money has no longterm value"
---

Money and possessions have no longterm value.

Our physical possessions have no longterm value. Without fail, they
leave us empty-handed. Perhaps we sell them or give them away. Perhaps
they are literally stolen from us, or perhaps time steals them away as
things wear down, rust, and decompose. Perhaps they sit in a spare room,
gathering dust until we die, at which point they stay here, abandoning
us as we head into life after death. Whatever their fate, the point is
that they do not continue to be valuable to us over the longterm.
Eighty years is a pretty small slice of eternity.

Money is similar. In fact, the case of money is even more hopeless.
Money is just a story we tell ourselves. Its value rises and falls and
fades away. The American Dollar didn\'t exist in Jesus\' day, and if we
transported one back, it might be a marvel to see such finely printed
paper, but it would have no value as currency. Today, a Roman coin is
similarly valuable not as currency, but as a curiosity and collectible.
The shells, grain, teeth, and minerals we have used as [money throughout
history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_money) are money no
longer.

Even at the height of some currency's power, that currency has little
real value. The parable of the Rich Fool shows that money can never save
us from death (Luke 12:16--21).The story of Lazarus and the Rich Man
shows that money is of no use after death, either (Luke 16:19-31),
consistent with the old saying: you'll never see a UHaul behind a
hearse. In life, money cannot buy peace with God, heal our inner
brokenness, or reform our character. Having more money does nothing in
and of itself to help us love God or what he has made.

That's not to say that money cannot be used for good, even long-lived
good. It certainly can. The money the Allies spent to stop Hitler was
money well-spent. The money Gutenberg spent while inventing the printing
press was money well-spent. In both situations, however, the wealth
doesn't lie in the money itself, but in what happened because of the way
the money was used.

The point is that money is a useful tool for storing and applying power
here on earth, but stockpiling it does nothing to solve the real
problems we face as humans. Real wealth lies not in amassing money, but
in something else entirely, something that can solve our problems.
