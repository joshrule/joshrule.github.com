title: "The economic analogy of a steward"
---

The New Testament frequently portrays followers of Jesus as stewards.

For example, Jesus tells this story in Luke 12:42--46, ""Who then is the
faithful and sensible steward, whom his master will put in charge of his
servants, to give them their rations at the proper time? Blessed is that
slave whom his master finds so doing when he comes. Truly I say to you
that he will put him in charge of all his possessions. But if that slave
says in his heart, 'My master will take a long time to come,' and he
begins to beat the other slaves, both men and women, and to eat and
drink and get drunk; then the master of that slave will come on a day
that he does not expect, and at an hour that he does not know, and will
cut him in two, and assign him a place with the unbelievers."

To give you some context, Jesus is talking to his followers here,
telling them that although he is gone now, there will come a day when he
returns to make all things right. Awesome! That is good news, because
there are things that need to be made right in this world.

Jesus then goes on to tell his followers that, if they are stewarding
the things he has left in their care, he will honor them. But, if we
abuse God's trust in us, he will respond by cutting us out of life with
him. Take a moment to reflect on that. Jesus is brutally honest here,
and it's worth your attention. How we handle stewardship is eternally
significant. Getting it right means continuing to walk with God with
increased power to use his resources to manage his household. Getting it
wrong means death.

Now, the idea of being a steward is something we hear about mainly in
the context of environmentalism (like the Forest Stewardship Council),
church (maybe a stewardship drive), and epic fantasy (in the *The Lord
of the Rings*, Denethor is the steward of the kingdom of Gondor). But,
what is stewardship? Well, stewardship is a fiduciary relationship,
which just means that it is one where the steward manages an owner's
resources so as to promote the owner's best interests. The steward is
not the owner. The steward is in the service of the owner, taking care
of the owner's things for the good of the owner.

When we begin following Jesus, we take on the role of steward in his
household (Steward is just one of many roles we accept---adopted son;
friend; heir, servant/slave; [ambassador]({% post_url
2026-01-15-the-political-analogy-of-an-ambassador%}) and others---but it is
definitely one of them.). We accept responsibility to manage God's resources so
as to promote his best interests. Now, because [God owns everything]({% post_url
2026-01-15-God-owns-everything %}), our role as steward essentially boils down
to using all the influence we have over every resource we have to carry out
God's purposes here on the earth. As we do this, we turn our life into an
outpost of the Kingdom of Heaven, where we serve as God's steward and [agent]({%
post_url 2026-01-15-how-ownership-works-we-are-gods-agents %}).
